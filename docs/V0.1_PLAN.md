# Startup Arena — v0.1 Plan

**Status:** Implemented locally on August 21, 2026; see `../README.md` and `DEVELOPMENT.md`  
**Goal:** Validate one daily startup battle and one daily challenger auction.  
**Initial arena:** One global arena; no categories.

## 1. v0.1 in one sentence

Every 24 hours, the current champion battles the founder who won the previous day's public auction; the audience votes, the winner becomes champion, and a new auction opens for the next challenger.

## 2. What v0.1 must prove

The MVP is successful only if it demonstrates all four of these behaviors:

1. People understand the battle and vote without explanation.
2. Founders share their battle pages with their own audiences.
3. The battle produces measurable outbound traffic for both startups.
4. Founders compete in the next-challenger auction with real money.

The first version is not intended to prove that Startup Arena can become a large directory, ranking authority, or ad network.

## 3. Core product rules

### Battle

- There is one active battle at a time.
- A battle lasts 24 hours.
- The battle question is: **“Which would you rather use?”**
- Each battle has exactly two startups: the incumbent champion and the challenger.
- Users cast one vote per battle.
- The startup with the most valid votes wins.
- The winner becomes the next champion and receives a free return battle.
- A tie is resolved in favor of the incumbent champion.
- There is no maximum win streak in v0.1.

### Auction

- One auction runs during each active battle for the following day's challenger slot.
- The auction is public and shows the current highest bid.
- The highest valid bidder at auction close wins the challenger slot.
- The winner pays their final bid; losing bidders pay nothing.
- A new bid must exceed the current highest bid by at least the minimum increment.
- Suggested initial minimum bid: **$5**.
- Suggested initial minimum increment: **$1**.
- The current champion cannot bid against their own title.
- Only approved startups may bid.
- If no valid bid exists, the admin selects a free wildcard challenger so the daily battle can continue.

### Payment principle

Founders pay for the right to enter a specific, measurable 24-hour battle—not for votes or a guaranteed win.

The payment and battle rules must be visible from the homepage and rules page:

> Money buys entry, not victory.

## 4. Daily operating cycle

Use fixed UTC boundaries so the schedule is deterministic.

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 00:00 | Current battle starts; previous battle is finalized |
| 00:00–23:00 | Voting and next-challenger bidding are open |
| 23:00 | Auction closes; no further bids are accepted |
| 23:00–00:00 | Payment settlement, moderation, and next battle preparation |
| 00:00 | New battle begins |

The one-hour settlement window gives the system time to validate the winner's payment and gives an admin a way to repair a failed transition during the early MVP.

### Battle finalization sequence

1. Stop accepting votes.
2. Mark the battle as ended.
3. Apply basic vote-abuse checks.
4. Calculate the final valid vote totals.
5. Determine the winner using the tie rule.
6. Update the champion and streak.
7. Close the auction.
8. Settle the winning payment.
9. Create the next battle.
10. Publish the result and share card.

Every step must be safe to retry. A failed job must not create two winners, charge twice, or create duplicate battles.

## 5. MVP user experiences

### Voter

1. Land on the homepage.
2. See the champion, challenger, countdown, and two symmetrical startup cards.
3. Open either or both startup websites in a new tab.
4. Choose which product they would rather use.
5. Authenticate with email or an existing account if required.
6. Cast one vote.
7. See the current vote share and outbound links.
8. Optionally share the battle or follow the next result.

Keep voting friction low, but do not make voting completely anonymous. The minimum defensible rule is one verified account per battle, plus rate limiting and bot protection.

### Founder

1. Submit a startup URL and basic product information.
2. Wait for admin approval.
3. Open the next-challenger auction.
4. Enter a bid and provide a payment method.
5. See whether the startup is currently leading.
6. If successful, receive a confirmation and battle page.
7. Share the battle with the founder's audience.
8. View basic results after the battle: impressions, clicks, votes, vote share, and cost per click.

The founder's startup should get value whether it wins or loses. Both cards receive equal visual treatment, an outbound link, and basic analytics.

### Admin

The admin dashboard must support:

- approving or rejecting startup submissions;
- editing startup copy and images;
- creating the initial seeded battle;
- viewing current bids and payment status;
- pausing an auction or battle;
- disqualifying a startup or suspicious votes;
- retrying finalization;
- selecting a wildcard challenger;
- viewing clicks, votes, and error logs.

Manual admin controls are acceptable in v0.1. They are part of the operating model, not a failure of automation.

## 6. v0.1 screens

### Public screens

- `/` — active battle, countdown, voting, auction status, recent result
- `/battle/:id` — permanent battle result page with shareable URL
- `/submit` — founder submission form
- `/rules` — battle, auction, voting, payment, and moderation rules
- `/about` — concise explanation of the product

### Founder screens

- `/account` — submitted startups, current bids, and results
- `/startup/:slug` — startup's public history and basic stats

### Admin screens

- `/admin/submissions`
- `/admin/battle`
- `/admin/auction`
- `/admin/moderation`

Do not build a separate marketing site, mobile app, category system, comments, tournaments, recommendations, or investor features in v0.1.

## 7. Startup submission fields

Required:

- startup name;
- canonical website URL;
- one-sentence pitch;
- founder email;
- launch status;
- confirmation that the product is functional and safe to link.

Optional:

- logo;
- screenshot;
- demo URL;
- founder social profile.

The admin can correct presentation details before approval. The public battle card should stay compact and symmetrical:

- logo;
- name;
- one-line pitch;
- screenshot or preview;
- “Visit startup” button;
- vote button.

## 8. Payment and auction behavior

For v0.1, use a straightforward first-price auction:

- the winner pays the amount they bid;
- bids are public;
- losing bidders are not charged;
- bids are final after the auction closes;
- a cancelled battle triggers a refund for the winning challenger;
- a failed winning payment makes the next-highest valid bidder eligible.

To avoid fake bids, a bidder must have a verified payment method before their bid is displayed as valid. The implementation should authorize or verify the payment method when bidding and capture only the winning amount after auction close.

All payment webhooks must be idempotent. The database, not the browser or Stripe redirect, is the source of truth for auction status.

## 9. Voting and trust model

### Minimum controls

- one vote per verified account per battle;
- unique database constraint on `(battle_id, user_id)`;
- email verification or magic-link authentication;
- rate limiting by account, IP hash, and device/session signals;
- Cloudflare Turnstile or equivalent on suspicious activity;
- founder/admin accounts flagged for review;
- manual ability to invalidate suspicious votes;
- public explanation of the rules.

Founder campaigns are allowed. Automated voting, purchased votes, and vote farms are not.

The product should initially display percentages rather than raw vote counts before a user votes, reducing bandwagon effects. After voting, the user can see the current result.

## 10. Suggested technical architecture

Because the repository is currently empty, use one full-stack application for v0.1:

- **Frontend/backend:** Next.js with TypeScript and the App Router
- **Styling:** Tailwind CSS
- **Database:** PostgreSQL
- **ORM:** Drizzle or Prisma
- **Authentication:** email magic links through a managed auth provider
- **Payments:** Stripe PaymentIntents or SetupIntents with webhook handling
- **Scheduling:** one managed cron job invoking an idempotent finalization endpoint
- **Bot protection:** Cloudflare Turnstile
- **Image storage:** managed object storage for logos and screenshots
- **Hosting:** one managed deployment platform plus managed PostgreSQL

Do not introduce a separate API, Redis, WebSockets, queue system, or worker service until traffic or reliability requires it. Live vote totals can use polling in v0.1.


### Startup

```text

name
```

### Battle

```text

starts_at
ends_at
status               // scheduled, live, ended, validating, finalized, cancelled
winner_startup_id
champion_votes
challenger_votes
finalized_at
```

### Auction

```text
id
closes_at
status               // open, closed, settling, awarded, no_bid, cancelled
winning_bid_id
created_at
```

### Bid

```text
id
auction_id
startup_id
user_id
amount_cents
status               // valid, outbid, winning, payment_failed, cancelled
payment_reference
created_at
```

### Vote

```text
id
battle_id
user_id
startup_id
created_at
ip_hash
fraud_status         // pending, valid, invalid, review
```

Add a unique constraint on `(battle_id, user_id)` and indexes on battle status, auction close time, startup owner, and bid amount.

### Event

```text
id
event_type           // impression, card_view, outbound_click, vote, share
battle_id
startup_id
user_id_optional
metadata_json
created_at
```

An event table is sufficient for v0.1 analytics. Do not build a separate event warehouse.

## 12. Implementation order

### Milestone 0 — Manual validation

- Recruit 4–8 startups.
- Create one battle manually.
- Use a simple form or prototype to test the wording and card layout.
- Confirm founders will share their battle.
- Measure whether people vote and click through.

### Milestone 1 — Battle engine

- Create the Next.js application.
- Add the database schema and seed data.
- Render two startup cards.
- Add authenticated voting and duplicate-vote prevention.
- Add battle countdown and result page.
- Implement finalization and winner-stays-on logic.
- Add admin controls and an audit log.

### Milestone 2 — Submission and auction

- Add founder authentication.
- Add startup submission and admin approval.
- Add one active auction.
- Add public bid display and bid validation.
- Add Stripe payment-method verification.
- Capture only the winning bid.
- Connect the auction winner to the next battle.

### Milestone 3 — Analytics and launch hardening

- Track impressions, card views, clicks, votes, and shares.
- Add founder results pages.
- Add basic abuse controls.
- Add failure alerts for scheduled jobs and payment webhooks.
- Add shareable battle/result cards.
- Publish rules, privacy policy, and payment/refund terms.

## 13. Launch scope checklist

### Must have

- one live battle;
- one next-challenger auction;
- founder submission and approval;
- authenticated one-vote-per-battle rule;
- winner finalization;
- Stripe payment flow;

## 14. Success metrics

Track every battle:

### Audience

- unique battle visitors;
- vote conversion rate;
- percentage who view both cards;
- outbound clicks per startup;
- repeat visitors within seven days;
- share rate.

### Founder marketplace

- approved startups;
- valid bidders per auction;
- auction fill rate;
- winning bid;
- repeat bidder rate;
- cost per outbound click;
- founder-reported satisfaction;
- payment failure and dispute rate.

### Suggested early validation targets

These are decision-making targets, not promises:

- at least 100 independent voters per battle;
- at least 2 valid bidders in most auctions;
- at least 10% seven-day voter return;
- at least 20% of participating founders share their battle;
- at least one repeat bidder after the first few battles;
- measurable outbound clicks for both the champion and challenger.

If people vote but founders do not bid, improve audience quality and reporting. If founders bid but people do not return, improve the battle experience before adding more monetization.

## 15. Major risks and planned responses

### No challenger bids

Seed the first battles manually. Keep a wildcard queue of approved startups. Do not let an empty auction produce a broken homepage.

### Founder pays and gets no value

Show impressions, clicks, votes, and vote share. Give both participants equal card treatment. Keep early minimum bids low.

### Vote manipulation

Require verified voting, rate-limit aggressively, and retain the ability to invalidate votes. Do not claim that the winner is objectively the better startup; call it the community battle winner.

### Champion dominates indefinitely

Monitor streak length and return behavior. Do not add a retirement rule until the data shows that a dominant champion hurts engagement.

### Payment or job failure

Use explicit state machines, database transactions, idempotency keys, and an admin retry path. Every daily transition should be observable and repairable.

## 16. v0.1 product promise

Startup Arena should make one promise clearly:

> Every day, one startup gets challenged, the audience decides the winner, and the next challenger earns their place through a transparent auction.

That is enough product for the first real test.
