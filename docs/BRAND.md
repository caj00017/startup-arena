# Startup Arena Brand System

## Core signal

Startup Arena's identifying color is **Signal Blue (`#1677FF`)**.

The color has a semantic job, not merely a decorative one. Signal Blue means an element is live, selected, actionable, or part of the active arena. Repetition should let users recognize that state before reading its label—the same kind of learned product distinction created by a consistently colored message bubble or status indicator.

## Why blue

Color psychology does not establish a universally most attractive hex value. Preferences vary with context, culture, surrounding colors, lightness, saturation, and personal experience. The evidence does, however, support a direction:

- Palmer and Schloss found broad preference for blue and proposed that color preference reflects associations with positively valued objects such as clear sky and clean water ([PNAS, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0906172107)).
- Blue was generally associated with positive emotions in a color-patch study, despite the English metaphor linking blue and sadness ([Jonauskaite et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7027086/)).
- In an affect and reward-system experiment, blue was rated as more pleasant and less arousing than red ([Kraus et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7311418/)).
- Color-pair research found that combinations sharing a hue but differing in lightness tend to feel more harmonious and preferable ([Schloss and Palmer, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3037488/)).

Signal Blue is therefore a product choice informed by research, not a claim that science identified `#1677FF` specifically. It is vivid enough for competition and momentum while retaining blue's broadly pleasant, dependable character.

## Palette

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal Blue | `#1677FF` | Live states, selected states, primary branded surfaces |
| Deep Blue | `#0756C7` | Links, focus, shadows, and blue text on light surfaces |
| Night Blue | `#082B66` | Auction and immersive dark surfaces |
| Soft Blue | `#DDEBFF` | Success, information, and low-emphasis branded surfaces |
| Ink | `#0B1220` | Primary text, borders, and text over Signal Blue |
| Canvas | `#F5F8FC` | Page background |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Cards and controls |
| Signal Red | `#EF5B4C` | Errors, destructive actions, and exceptional urgency only |

## Usage rules

1. Use Signal Blue only when an element carries product meaning: live, selected, actionable, or active.
2. Use Deep Blue for normal-size text on white or Canvas. Signal Blue itself is a surface color, not body-link text.
3. Use white text inside Signal Blue pills, buttons, and circular badges. Keep longer text on Paper or Canvas surfaces.
4. Use white text on Night Blue. This pairing has a contrast ratio of approximately `13.57:1`.
5. Build hierarchy with lighter and darker versions of the blue rather than introducing competing hues.
6. Reserve Signal Red for errors and warnings; never use it as a second brand accent.
7. Never rely on color alone. Live, success, payment, and error states must retain text or icon labels.

## Validation

The palette is a strong default, not a substitute for observing users. During the pilot, compare recognition of active states, perceived trust, and unaided color recall. Keep the hue stable during that test so the team can learn whether Signal Blue acquires the intended product meaning.
